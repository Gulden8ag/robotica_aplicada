## Solution Methods

>> Every system that uses revolute and prismatic joints with a total of 6 degrees of freedom (DOF) has a unique solution to the inverse kinematics problem.

!!! Warning "Note"
    This only applies to numerical methods, as analytical and geometric methods may have multiple solutions or no solution at all depending on the manipulator's configuration and the desired end-effector pose. 




- **Analytical methods:** These methods involve deriving closed-form equations for the joint angles based on the end-effector position and orientation. They are computationally efficient but may not always be possible for complex manipulators.

- **Numerical methods:** These methods involve iterative algorithms to find the joint angles that minimize the error between the desired and actual end-effector position and orientation. They are more flexible but can be computationally expensive.

- **Geometric methods:** These methods involve using geometric relationships between the joints and the end-effector to derive the joint angles. They can be computationally efficient but may not always be possible for complex manipulators.


## Analytical methods

Let's create an example of an analytical method for a simple 2 DOF planar manipulator.

**Problem Setup (2R planar arm)**

- Link lengths: \(l_1\) and \(l_2\)
- Joint angles: \(\theta_1\) and \(\theta_2\)
- End-effector position: \((x, y)\)

### 1. Define the forward kinematics of the manipulator:

Using whichever method you prefer, we can derive the forward kinematics of the 2R planar arm:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= l_1 \cos\theta_1 + l_2 \cos(\theta_1 + \theta_2) \\
y &= l_1 \sin\theta_1 + l_2 \sin(\theta_1 + \theta_2)
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's give some numerical values to the link lengths for our example:

- \(l_1 = 1\) 
- \(l_2 = 1\)
- Target end-effector position: \((x, y) = (1, 1)\)

### 2. Check reachability:

Compute the distance to the target:

$$
r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = \sqrt{1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.414
$$

A 2-link arm can reach if:
$$
|l_1 - l_2| \leq r \leq l_1 + l_2
$$

Here:
$$
0 \leq \sqrt{2} \leq 2 \rightarrow \text{reachable}
$$

### 3. SOlve for \(\theta_2\), using the law of cosines:

For a 2-link triangle:

$$
\cos\theta_2 = \frac{x^2 + y^2 - l_1^2 - l_2^2}{2 l_1 l_2}
$$

Substituting the values:
$$
\cos\theta_2 = \frac{1^2 + 1^2 - 1^2 - 1^2}{2 \cdot 1 \cdot 1} = \frac{0}{2} = 0
$$

Thus:
$$
\theta_2 = \arccos(0) = \frac{\pi}{2} or -\frac{\pi}{2}
$$

### 4. Solve for \(\theta_1\):

A standard closed-form expression is:

$$
\theta_1 = \arctan2(y, x) - \arctan2(l_2 \sin\theta_2, l_1 + l_2 \cos\theta_2)
$$

First, compute \(\arctan2(y, x)\):
$$
\arctan2(1, 1) = \frac{\pi}{4}
$$

Then we have two possible values for \(\theta_2\):

CASE A: \(\theta_2 = \frac{\pi}{2}\)

- \(\sin\theta_2 = 1\)
- \(\cos\theta_2 = 0\)

$$
\arctan2(l_2 \sin\theta_2, l_1 + l_2 \cos\theta_2) = \arctan2(1, 1) = \frac{\pi}{4}
$$

Thus:
$$
\theta_1 = \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{\pi}{4} = 0
$$
CASE B: \(\theta_2 = -\frac{\pi}{2}\)
- \(\sin\theta_2 = -1\)
- \(\cos\theta_2 = 0\)
$$
\arctan2(l_2 \sin\theta_2, l_1 + l_2 \cos\theta_2) = \arctan2(-1, 1) = -\frac{\pi}{4}
$$
Thus:
$$
\theta_1 = \frac{\pi}{4} - (-\frac{\pi}{4}) = \frac{\pi}{2}
$$

### 5. Verify the solutions:

- For \(\theta_1 = 0\) and \(\theta_2 = \frac{\pi}{2}\):
$$
x = 1 \cdot \cos(0) + 1 \cdot \cos(0 + \frac{\pi}{2}) = 1 + 0 = 1
$$

$$
y = 1 \cdot \sin(0) + 1 \cdot \ sin(0 + \frac{\pi}{2}) = 0 + 1 = 1
$$

- For \(\theta_1 = \frac{\pi}{2}\) and \(\theta_2 = -\frac{\pi}{2}\):

$$
x = 1 \cdot \cos(\frac{\pi}{2}) + 1 \cdot \cos(\frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{\pi}{2}) = 0 + 1 = 1
$$
